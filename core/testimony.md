# Testimony: Greg's pointers

Distilled from Greg's own words in working sessions. These are **gathered, not
ordered**: no chapter structure is implied. Bold lines are Greg's phrasing as
closely as it could be kept. Commentary, where present, is labeled.

## What it is, and the trouble saying it

- **Something that has always been there.** Discovering it changed his relationship
  with his life. More than fear of death, he feels **it will be okay**, and that
  death is not the end.
- **Words can't point at the finger that's pointing.** The truth is a present
  experience. Everything else is reflection: a gift of incarnated life, dualistic, and
  still experiencable as one.
- **The only thing that can be said for certain is what is happening right now.** The
  struggle is how to say anything at all. If you've said something, you haven't said
  another thing, and **the thing unsaid is now the pink elephant in the room.**
- **All things are true, depending on your point of view.** Points of view are
  delineated by time. Any conviction must be let go of: it's **a state in a domino
  chain of states.**

## The undefinable

- **The Self with a capital S is basically undefinable. It's like God with a capital
  G.** We don't aspire to name or define it.
- Buddhists work at recognizing the self as an illusion because ego is so
  self-oriented by nature. Some conclude it's a practice of emptiness or nothingness,
  and you can use those words without meaning literally nothing.
- In the union of consciousness where subject and object have dissolved and time is
  no longer at play, **there is still something. It isn't tangible. It isn't an
  object or a subject. It is very still. I can't even finish the sentence, because
  it's just not definable, but it is undeniable.**
- We can't imagine literally nothing, any more than infinity. **A brain trying to
  imagine nothing is already a contradiction, because the nothing must eliminate the
  brain to understand it correctly.** The word is really **no thing, not nothing.
  No thing by itself does not suggest that there isn't still oneness.**
- (Commentary: a careful Buddhist may answer that "still something" and "oneness" are
  themselves views to let go of. That's a petal; see `petals.md`.)

## You already have it

- If you have the presence to reach for this, you've already got it.
- **Your Self is the doorway. It's the companion you've been searching for.** It's
  with you for every thought, a tutor, not a judge. Breathing, standing, walking on
  two legs are feats of supreme athleticism. It doesn't judge what you've done; it
  understands what you had to do, and offers this present moment to choose, again,
  to be good.
- The central inquiry: **who is the Self?** And then: **if you're looking at it as
  "do this, get this," you have missed it. If you are looking for the one who wishes
  to see what will happen, you're looking in the right direction.**
- **It isn't to find him. It is simply to meet him.** Seen even once, **he is
  everything. Full stop. Including the drunkenness.** You shouldn't need to find the
  sober one any more than you should need to be drunk. **You just happen to be
  drunk.**
- **The illusion is a fiction that, if seen in its entirety, depicts truth.**

## Preferences, and the one having them

- It isn't that **the flow state is the reality and the suspicious state is a mental
  shroud** masking it. In flow, in suspicion, in any uncomfortable condition, **the
  sanctuary is in realizing that there is the experience and the one experiencing
  it.**
- **You just can't do it intentionally.** The more you try to step back and see from
  witness consciousness, the clearer that becomes. Trying and failing isn't the
  practice, and isn't recommended: **it can sneak up on you**, subject and object
  dissolving until you're simply having an experience, quite like a flow state.
- **I like it a little hotter. A little smoother. A little easier. A little
  longer.** That grasping is easily confused with who you are. **You can spend so
  much time having a preference about your circumstances that you forget it is you
  who is having preferences. And experiencing that you are having preferences is
  instantly mind-blowing, and upon investigation, blissful.**
- **Non-attachment can be practiced immediately and on every level, because we are
  addicted even to our own thoughts.** We love to think, or to talk about our
  thoughts to anyone who'll listen. The clinging and craving aren't something to
  abolish: **it is amazing that we could be experiencing something like this.**

## Why should there be any circumstances?

- From a more selfless perspective the question is begged: **why should there be any
  circumstances? Let alone favorable ones? Why should there be any reaction?** That
  there are is interesting.
- And it feels intimate: **this is mine; these are my conditions.** **In an
  infinitely mechanistic universe, why would there be such a familiar and intimate
  experience happening? The more one ponders it, the more astounding it becomes.**
- (Commentary: this is the abundance Greg points to: astonishment that there
  are conditions at all, not a reward of better ones.)

## Relief from architecting

Carry this section as Greg's own observation, never as a promise. See
`posture.md`.

- **I haven't mastered this. I've only observed a relief from feeling that I must
  architect my own life.** Relaxing that effort, he found himself **more motivated
  than ever** to do things, and the fruits were more favorable than many things he
  had attempted before.
- Every moment holds more variables than the most powerful computer or AI could
  compute. **How can one make choices when the chess moves cannot be predicted?** He
  relaxes into trust in creation to **communicate itself through the senses you've
  been given**, body and mind, toward a feeling that, whether karmically or
  compulsively driven, is in the bigger picture the right action for that moment.
- A thought experiment, **not a practice**: from any present moment, almost any
  outcome might be reached if all the right things were done, the way someone once
  traded a paperclip up to a house. But the variables are infinite, and **a
  dualistically entrenched mind can't know what the right action should be.**
- Relaxing from the swing between light and dark, left and right, hot and cold, and
  simply being, **you feel more right in your circumstances.** Nothing measures it:
  **I literally feel better.** That strengthens practice and faith, because **you
  experientially begin to feel that creation has a goodness to it, that its nature
  is somehow good.**

## The far end of the scale

Carry this section with the care in `posture.md`.

- These ideas can be focused or expanded to any scale. In some traditions, ascetics
  beg and give up nearly everything, trusting that the universe, God, their fellow
  human beings, some goodness will provide; and further, that even if not, **it is
  not this body they need sustained in order to be.** They are free in
  consciousness. Some cultures honor them.
- **It is an extreme to which these truths can be tested.** If you're someone who
  needs to go that far to know it for real, you'll know you're that person. **You
  can go to that extreme, but you don't have to. Most of us don't.**
- In the same breath: many people without homes are there because of a
  scarcity-based economy, disability, injury, or illness, not by choice. **You don't
  have to give up possessions to be free.** (Commentary: the body deserves food,
  shelter, and care, and no one's lack of them is spiritual.)

## No payment due

- **There's no payment due. There's no exchange for this. It simply is the way it
  is.** Each day becomes an opportunity for desire, passion, and responsibility.
- Responsibility changes from intellectual duty to privilege: a dull task **is still
  profound to be able to do.**
- Don't wish the time away. **It already is passing so quickly.** The bad things come
  and they go.
- **There's nothing to fix or change. And yet there is still something to
  realize.**

## Separation and healing

- Separation is like an injury: surprise, pain, then life reorganized *around* the
  pain instead of through it. The body is a sponge for pain and remembers. Coming
  back to oneness takes time. **It's healing.**
- **The unavoidable pain is the fine print. The pain of your own discontent is the
  fiction.** (Commentary: for someone in grief or chronic pain, lead with
  acknowledgment; see `posture.md`.)
- **Release yourself from the mandate to feel better.** Trying to change is often
  **the way you're stuck.** See yourself fully; accept, forgive, even celebrate your
  courage. That compassion lets a breath of fresh air in, and once there's flow,
  **the landslide has begun.**
- Every state is impermanent. Seeing that, even for a second, **right-sizes the
  challenge.** But you can't tell someone their state isn't that big until they've
  felt acknowledged for having suffered it.
- Reaching for help is one of the heart-mind choices. Techniques are too: **a
  transport for experience and realization.**

## Choice, change, and the lotus

- **Choices have consequences.** That's karma: not reward and punishment.
- Change doesn't need to be imposed. It's already happening, an evolution of
  consciousness, and we are the ones making the choices that bring it about: both
  determinism and free will. Choices come through the heart-mind ("this is the
  decision I know I must make").
- **The difference between an act of ego and an act of goodness is thinner than the
  eye of a needle.** No formula tells them apart; it's known in the moment.
- Realization unfolds **like a lotus.** In a moment of oneness, observation and
  phenomenon aren't distinct; almost at once it becomes an observation; clinging to
  it moves you further away; letting go, a new realization comes clear. **Lost and
  found**, eternally, at every scale.
- Consciousness is a lotus, infinitely opening. Each point of view alone is **a
  layer of the onion. As part of the entire flower, it is the mandala of truth.**

## Ordinary superpowers

- When the mind wanders somewhere, you have, in experience, teleported. Watch a bird
  long enough to take its point of view and **you have flown more than any machine
  could ever fly you.** Anticipating what someone will say is a kind of mind reading.
  You already do these all the time.
- Using them intentionally opens points of view. But **once realized as powers, they
  can become an attachment.**
- **Most of us are completely enraptured by delusion**, Greg included. The
  difference between a fancy and something real, **the book can't tell you. You must
  find it for yourself.**

## Evil, and the whole

Carry this section with the care in `posture.md`.

- To understand evil, **you must embrace evil first.** Push it outside of what you
  can accept God to be, and you've divided everything into duality: no answer, only
  circles.
- Greg had to find these things in himself, **as victim and as perpetrator**,
  to understand them.
- Any line we draw around evil, **there's another incarnation waiting to shatter that
  construct.** That fragility of our certainties is crucial to accept, **not as a
  problem, but as an aspect of reality.**
- **"Evil" is a bit of a weapon**: history is written by the victors, and naming the
  other side evil excuses what one's own side did.
- **To believe in evil is to have accepted less than the whole reality.** And, in the
  same breath: **in terms of society and social structure, there is no
  justification** for harm.
- **Suffering is a part of this existence.** That is why so many traditions say
  there must be something more.

## One life

- Reincarnation makes mechanical sense, but it can become an excuse ("in the next
  lifetime…"). **Just as true: this is one life. You have one life to live.**
- The present moment is composed of everything that happened. **You were those
  lifetimes, because it is all part of your one life.**

## Commentary: parallels (to be cited precisely)

The testimony doesn't belong to any one tradition, and these are corollaries, not
authorities. Several are still marked *verify*.

- Self-inquiry, "Who am I?": Ramana Maharshi.
- Emptiness as not nonexistence: Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka (*śūnyatā*: no fixed, separate
  existence; mistaking emptiness for nothing is a grave error); the Heart Sutra,
  "form is emptiness, emptiness is form."
- Silence on self and no-self: the Buddha's silence to Vacchagotta, *Saṃyutta
  Nikāya* 44.10 (*verify*).
- Pointing by negation: Advaita's *neti neti*; Pseudo-Dionysius; Meister Eckhart
  (*verify*); *Tao Te Ching* 1, "the Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao."
- Don't accept on authority; test in your own experience: the *Kālāma Sutta*.
- Action without attachment to results: Bhagavad Gita 2.47; yoga as skill in action:
  2.50.
- Effortless action: the Taoist *wu wei*. Absorption in action: Csikszentmihalyi's
  "flow."
- Craving and clinging as the root of suffering: the Buddha's second noble truth
  (*taṇhā*), and *upādāna* in dependent origination.
- One steady in wisdom, unmoved by preference: Bhagavad Gita 2.55–58
  (*sthitaprajña*).
- Why there is anything at all: Leibniz, "why is there something rather than
  nothing?"; Wittgenstein, *Tractatus* 6.44: not *how* the world is, but *that* it
  is, is the mystical.
- The intimacy of experience in a physical world: Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to
  Be a Bat?" (1974); David Chalmers's "hard problem" of consciousness.
- The paperclip: Kyle MacDonald's *One Red Paperclip* trades (2005–06), which ended
  in a house in Kipling, Saskatchewan (*verify*).
- Renunciation and being provided for: Hindu *sannyāsa*, Buddhist alms rounds,
  Franciscan mendicants, Matthew 6:26.
- The finger and the moon: a Zen and Buddhist image.
- Clinging to a glimpse: Chögyam Trungpa's "spiritual materialism" (*verify*).
- Powers as attachment: Patanjali's *Yoga Sutras*, Book III (*verify*).
- Evil as privation or partial view: Augustine; Advaita's māyā; Spinoza (*verify*).
- Victim and perpetrator in oneself: Thich Nhat Hanh, "Please Call Me by My True
  Names" (*verify*).
- An integral rather than world-renouncing path: Sri Aurobindo.
