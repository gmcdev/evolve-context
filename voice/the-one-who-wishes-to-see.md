# The one who wishes to see

*A passage in Greg's voice, built from his own phrases by his co-author and
kept by Greg. For the reader who wants proof. Provisional.*

You want proof. Good. So did I.

Notice the shape of the wanting: *show me that if I do this, I'll get that.* It's a
fair request. It's how we've learned nearly everything worth knowing: how to build a
bridge, set a bone, grow food.

I can't give you that here. Not because I'm hiding it, but because the moment I
handed it to you, it would be a recipe, and you'd be busy cooking instead of
looking. If sitting quietly reliably produced this, everyone would sit, everyone
would have it, and there would be no game, no mystery, no play.

So I'll ask something stranger. Don't look at what you might get. Look at the one
who wants it.

Right now, as you read, part of you is weighing each sentence. *Does this hold up?
Is he selling something? Is this nonsense?* Stay with that for a moment. Not the
verdict. The one weighing.

Watch yourself watching. Then watch that.

It doesn't argue. It doesn't need this to be right or wrong. It was here before you
picked this up, and it will be here when you put it down, attending to every thought
you have, the clever ones and the foolish ones alike, without keeping score.

I can't prove that to you. I can only tell you that I've looked, day after day, and
it has always been there.

I'm not a master. I'm a practitioner. But I'll say this much: the one asking for
proof is closer to it than any proof could bring you. You don't have to believe me.
You only have to look.

**Provenance.** Greg's phrases: "do this, get this"; "no game, no mystery, no
play"; "watch yourself watching yourself, and then watch that"; the Self attending
to every thought without judging; "I am but a practitioner." Co-author's: the bridge,
bone, and food; "busy cooking instead of looking"; "without keeping score"; the
